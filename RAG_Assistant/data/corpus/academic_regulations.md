# Academic Regulations

## Credit Load and Course Levels
A full-time undergraduate student at Halcyon Ridge University (HRU) carries between 12 and 18 credits per term. A full-time graduate student carries at least 9 credits per term, and graduate students may register for no more than 15 credits in a term.

Undergraduates who want to register for more than 18 credits (an overload) need written approval from their academic dean and must hold a cumulative GPA of at least 3.3. No undergraduate may register for more than 21 credits in any term, regardless of GPA.

Courses numbered 100-299 are lower-division, courses numbered 300-499 are upper-division, and courses numbered 500 and above are graduate courses.

## Grading Scale
HRU uses the following letter grades. Each letter grade carries grade points that are averaged into the GPA.

| Letter grade | Grade points | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| A | 4.0 | 93-100 |
| A- | 3.7 | 90-92 |
| B+ | 3.3 | 87-89 |
| B | 3.0 | 83-86 |
| B- | 2.7 | 80-82 |
| C+ | 2.3 | 77-79 |
| C | 2.0 | 73-76 |
| C- | 1.7 | 70-72 |
| D | 1.0 | 60-69 |
| F | 0.0 | below 60 |

The lowest passing grade for undergraduate courses is D. Graduate students must earn a B- or higher for a course to count toward a graduate degree; a grade of C+ or lower earns no degree credit, although it is still included in the graduate GPA.

Undergraduates may take up to 12 credits on a Pass/Fail basis outside their major. A Pass requires a grade of C or higher and does not affect the GPA.

## Academic Standing and Probation
A student is in good standing when the cumulative GPA is at least 2.0 (undergraduate) or 3.0 (graduate). A student whose cumulative GPA falls below the applicable threshold is placed on academic probation at the end of the term.

Students on probation must meet with their academic advisor within 10 days of the notice, may not register for more than 13 credits, and must return to good standing within two consecutive terms. A student who has not returned to good standing after two consecutive terms of probation is dismissed for one year and may apply for readmission afterwards.

The Dean's List recognizes undergraduates who earn a term GPA of 3.7 or higher while completing at least 12 graded credits.

## Repeating Courses
Undergraduates may repeat a course in which they earned a grade of C- or lower. A course may be attempted at most three times (the original attempt plus two repeats). When a course is repeated, only the most recent grade is counted in the GPA, up to a maximum of 12 repeated credits over the whole degree; every attempt remains visible on the transcript.

Graduate students may repeat a course only with the written approval of their program director, and in that case both grades count toward the graduate GPA.

## Incomplete Grades
An instructor may award a grade of Incomplete (I) when a student has finished at least 60% of the coursework and cannot complete the rest because of a documented emergency. The student and the instructor sign an Incomplete Contract that lists the remaining work and a due date.

All Incomplete grades must be resolved within 8 weeks after the start of the next regular term. Any Incomplete that is not resolved by then automatically becomes an F. The summer term does not count as a regular term.

## Grade Appeals
A student who disagrees with a final course grade must first discuss it with the instructor within 10 business days after the grade is posted. If the disagreement is not resolved, the student may submit a written appeal to the department chair within 30 calendar days after the grade is posted. The chair issues a decision within 15 business days.

The student may then appeal to the College Appeals Committee within 10 business days of the chair's decision; the committee's decision is final. Appeals may be based on calculation errors, deviation from the grading policy in the syllabus, or arbitrary grading, but not on disagreement with an instructor's academic judgment alone.

## Attendance and Final Examinations
Attendance rules are set by each instructor and must appear in the syllabus. An instructor may drop a student who misses the first two class meetings of a course without contacting the instructor (a "no-show drop").

Final examinations are held during a dedicated exam week at the end of each term. A student who is scheduled for three final examinations within a 24-hour period may petition the Registrar to move one of them; the petition is due by the end of week 12 of the term.
