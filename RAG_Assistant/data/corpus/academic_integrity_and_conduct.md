# Academic Integrity and Student Conduct

## What Counts as Academic Dishonesty
Academic dishonesty includes plagiarism (presenting another person's words or ideas as your own), fabrication of data or sources, cheating on examinations, unauthorized collaboration on graded work, submitting the same work in two courses without permission, and helping another student commit any of these acts.

## Generative AI Tools
Use of generative AI tools such as chatbots and code assistants is prohibited on graded work unless the course syllabus explicitly permits it. When a syllabus permits AI use, students must include an AI Use Statement that names the tool and describes how it was used. Undisclosed AI use is treated as academic dishonesty. Instructors may ask students to explain or reproduce their work orally.

## Sanctions
Sanctions escalate with each finding of responsibility. For a first minor offense, the student receives a grade of zero on the assignment and must complete the 2-hour Integrity Seminar. For a first major offense, such as cheating on an examination or fabricating data, the student receives an F in the course.

A second offense of any kind results in an F in the course and suspension for one full term. A third offense results in expulsion from the university. Sanctions are recorded in a confidential integrity file and do not appear on the transcript unless the student is suspended or expelled.

## Reporting and Hearing Process
An instructor who suspects a violation must report it to the Office of Academic Integrity within 5 business days of discovering it. The Office notifies the student in writing within 3 business days, and the student has 7 business days to respond. If the student contests the allegation, a hearing panel of two faculty members and one student is convened within 20 business days. The panel decides using the preponderance-of-the-evidence standard.

## Appeals of Integrity Decisions
A student may appeal a hearing panel's decision to the Provost's designee within 10 business days of the written decision. Appeals are accepted only on three grounds: a procedural error, new evidence that was not available at the hearing, or a sanction that is disproportionate to the violation. The Provost's designee decides within 30 days, and that decision is final.

## Self-Reporting
A student who reports their own violation to the Office of Academic Integrity before the instructor has discovered it receives a sanction one level lower than the standard sanction. Self-reporting is not available once an investigation has begun.

## Student Conduct Code
Non-academic misconduct, such as harassment, theft, damage to property or endangering others, is handled by the Office of Student Conduct. Sanctions range from a written warning to conduct probation, suspension and expulsion. Residence hall rules are covered in the Housing and Campus Life handbook.
